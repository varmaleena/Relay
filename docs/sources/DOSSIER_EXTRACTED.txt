1. Executive summary
Relay is an AI system that takes a scam victim's first, frightened report and turns it into the right
document packet for every institution that has to act: the victim's bank, the national cyber-fraud authority,
the payment app, and others. It prepares each packet in that institution's format, puts the contacts in the
right order, tracks deadlines, and follows each one to resolution. The rules for each country live in reviewed
data files called Jurisdiction Packs, so adding a country means adding a pack, not rewriting software.
Its tagline is "Guidance tells scam victims where to go. Relay does the work." Existing consumer
assistants mostly explain what to do. Our own small tests showed they stayed generic, did not name the
national channels, ignored urgency, and declined to draft the message a victim needs to send. Relay
closes that step.
Item Decision
Project name Relay (working title). The name comes from passing a case from one institution to the next like a
baton. An earlier name, "Golden Hour", was dropped because the phrase is already used by banks
and by Indian government guidance, so it cannot be owned.
Competition and Google Cloud AI Builder Cup 2026 (organized by Hack2skill, sponsored by Google Cloud). Theme:
theme BFSI: Intelligent Risk, Fraud and Financial Experiences.
Problem After a scam, the first hour decides whether money can be stopped, but victims face a maze of
institutions with different forms, orders, and deadlines that differ by country.
Solution Voice and chat intake in the victim's language; screenshot reading; classification (including
whether the victim authorized the payment); an ordered plan from a reviewed country pack;
verified, cited packets per institution; a receiver console; tracking.
Main users Scam victims (often elderly or not fluent in the official language); fraud and case staff at banks,
payment apps and authorities; compliance analysts who maintain country rules.
What is different It produces and tracks the packets each recipient needs, across institutions, in any country, with
sources and human approval, and it measures its own accuracy against a baseline.
Technology Google Gemini models (including the Live API for voice), Agent Development Kit, MCP and A2A,
Cloud Run, Firebase, Firestore, BigQuery, Vertex AI Search (now under Gemini Enterprise Agent
Platform).
Honest status Concept, research and full specification are done. No code has been written. Institutions in the
demo are simulated and labeled as such.
Estimated chances Gut estimate, not data: about 25 to 40% to be shortlisted for the Singapore finale and 6 to 12% to
reach the podium, if executed well.
The honest summary
Strongest points: the idea needs Gen AI (messy multilingual evidence, reasoning about authorization,
drafting, extracting rules from regulator text); packs make it generic and scalable; measured evaluation
against a baseline is rare in hackathon entries.
Weakest point: originality. A global assistant (Scam.org) and a bank-side Gen AI product (NICE
Actimize) overlap with parts of the idea. The differentiation rests on the cross-institution packets, the
packs, and tested gaps, so claims must stay narrow.
Biggest risks: legal accuracy of generated rules, a wide build that breaks live, and judges mistaking the
simulation for real integration.

4. The problem
4.1 Plain statement
When someone is scammed, speed decides whether money can be stopped. The victim is frightened, often
elderly, often not fluent in the official language, and often ashamed. To act, they must work out which
institutions to contact, in what order, with what information, in which format, by which deadline. That
differs by country and by type of payment. Mistakes are common and costly: the wrong portal category,
missing evidence, reporting only to the bank, or describing a payment the wrong way.
4.2 Why now
(cid:127) Scam losses are enormous. The Global Anti-Scam Alliance estimated about 442 billion USD lost to scams
across 42 surveyed countries in its 2025 report (Secondary, via press coverage).
(cid:127) Regulators are turning response into a duty. Singapore's Shared Responsibility Framework has been in
force since 16 Dec 2024; the UK has mandatory reimbursement for authorized push payment scams
since Oct 2024; Australia's Scams Prevention Framework includes a response principle with substantive
commencement on 31 Mar 2027. Institutions need fast, documented case handling.
(cid:127) Generative AI is changing both sides. Scams are becoming more personalized and convincing, and
institutions are expecting higher case volumes.

5. The solution: Relay
5.1 The idea in one paragraph
A victim speaks or types what happened, in Hindi or English, and uploads screenshots. Relay tells them the
first safe action within seconds, reads the evidence, decides what kind of scam it was and whether the
victim authorized the payment, finds the right country pack, and builds an ordered plan. For each
institution on the plan, it drafts a packet with the right fields, the right wording and citations to the rules it
used, and an independent checker verifies it. The victim reviews and approves each packet. Institution
staff see a complete, normalized case file in a console, decide what to do, and can ask the victim for
anything missing. Everything is tracked with deadlines and an audit trail.
1 Report 2 Plan 3 Review and send 4 Institution acts 5 Track
Victim speaks or types; Relay classifies and Victim checks each Organization gets a Status, deadlines,
uploads evidence orders who to contact message and approves complete case file requests for more info
The same five stages appear on every screen as a progress bar, so a viewer always knows where the case is.
5.2 The five parts

shows a plan as an ordered line of organizations; lets the victim edit and

5.3 A worked example

5.4 Key design ideas
(cid:127) Jurisdiction Packs are data. A pack lists who must be told, the channels, required fields, deadlines,
sequencing, liability descriptions, forms, languages and crisis resources, each with a source citation or
an "unverified" label. A new country is a new pack.
(cid:127) The Pack Builder. An agent reads a regulator's public guidance, extracts rules with passage
references, a critic verifies each one against its passage, and a human reviews and publishes. This is the
most original piece and the proof that "generic" is real.
(cid:127) Authorized versus unauthorized payments. The classifier separates a payment the victim made
because they were deceived from one made by someone else, because rules and remedies differ.
(cid:127) Rules are deterministic; the model writes and reads. The plan comes from a rule engine over the
pack. The model extracts messy input and writes the text, and a deterministic check confirms it adds
nothing the plan does not contain.
(cid:127) Verified and human-approved. Every packet is checked field by field and sentence by sentence;
every send, publish or decision needs an explicit human action.
(cid:127) Linked cases without sharing identities. Identifiers are stored only as keyed hashes. Links are
shown as "possible", explained by Gemini using only entity types, and confirmed by a person.
(cid:127) Honest simulation. Banks, authorities and payment apps are simulated and labeled on every relevant
screen.

13. Evaluation plan
The evaluation is a product feature, not an afterthought: it is what separates Relay from a demo.
System compared Definition
Relay Full pipeline with packs, agents and verifier
Baseline Same model, same facts, a general scam-guidance prompt with no packs, tools or rules

Dataset
At least 300 synthetic cases across the three reference packs and one live-built pack; English, Hindi and
Hinglish; 11 scam types; all payment-authorization types; noisy and incomplete inputs; text, screenshot
and transcript modalities; plus at least 40 injection cases. Screenshots are rendered from templates with
generic fake app skins. A 10% manual realism review and a dataset card record limits. All reports say the
data is synthetic.
